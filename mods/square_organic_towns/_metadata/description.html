Still being tuned, and I'd love your feedback. I have put many hours into Organic Towns so far: more than 50 test sessions in the game and over 800 towns grown and compared across seeds, climates, eras and name lists. I'm really excited to share it.

My goal was a game world that feels more alive: towns and cities that respond to the land around them, street names that say something about the place, and a unique, interesting experience in every new game.

Town growth is hard to get right on every map, so I'm still tuning it. Please tell me how it works on yours: post screenshots of your towns and cities in the comments, the good ones and the odd ones, along with your climate, name list and start year. Every report helps make the next update better.

---

Organic Towns: Cities that Grow makes towns grow the way real ones do: winding streets that follow the hills, rivers and shores, a shape and a character of their own, and new satellite towns out in the country as they grow. No more identical grids stamped onto every landscape.

Each town reads its site (flat and open, coast, river, hills, valley or ridge), its region and its era, and grows new streets to match. One map gets many looks: sprawling towns on big flat land, dense winding ones on the coast and in the hills, planned grids that become more common after 1910.

Features

  • Streets that fit the land: new streets follow the terrain, keep off steep slopes and water, and join the town at its dead ends. Towns grow along valleys, shores and the roads out of town instead of in round blobs.
  • Regional styles: European organic cores and 19th-century extensions, Mediterranean hill towns, Nordic towns, North American grids and suburbs, Japanese dense streets and superblocks. The map's name list picks the region, or choose one in the settings.
  • Every town its own form: clustered, radiating, long and narrow, fingers, sprawling with meadows between, or a strip along the shore. Neighboring towns are drawn to look different from each other.
  • Satellite towns: grown towns found small towns of their own nearby, linked by a road: residential districts on a hilltop, by the shore or across the water, and industrial Works by a harbor, a station or a factory. They are real towns with their own names and grow when you serve them.
  • Rivers, shores and bridges: river towns grow along their banks and now and then bridge the river to start a quarter on the far side. Many shore towns spread along the waterline like seaside resorts and lake towns.
  • Around the forest: many towns grow into open fields and meadows first and keep the woods around them. A few deep-forest towns wind their streets between the trees.
  • Landscape motifs: a street climbing the hillside, a fishbone along a valley, a curved harbor front, a village green, a ring road around the old core, a small grid quarter by your station.
  • Strong town characters: one to three towns per map on a striking site get a character that shapes all their growth: hilltop village, clifftop strip, harbor cove, street village, company town, garden city, island town, or a new town with roundabouts.
  • Landscape names: on a new map every town gets a name made from its own site, region and climate: "Two Waters" at a river bend, "Watersmeet" where two rivers meet, "Hornholt Isle" on a wooded spit, "Steinbach am Eichsee", "Pont-des-Saules", "Coral Key". Rivers and lakes are named too, and new road stops get a fitting street name. English, American, German, French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, Swedish, Norwegian and Japanese name lists.
  • Growth by deliveries: each town's mix of homes, shops and industry follows what you deliver, so a town fed with industrial cargo becomes a mill town and one served only by passenger lines stays mostly homes.
  • Towns mature: young towns stay low and spread out; grown towns build up, and about a third densify their center. From 1950 sprawling towns get wide arterials on their through routes.
  • An Organic Towns card in the town window: what is shaping the town: its mix of homes, shops and industry and where deliveries pull it, its character and form, its growth phase, its parent town and satellite towns (click one to go there), and why it got its name. A notification tells you when a town founds a new satellite town.
  • A head start: on a new map every town lays a few streets along its form in the first minutes, so towns differ from day one. Streets never run more than a block or two ahead of the houses.
  • Room for industry: towns prefer to grow away from heavy industry, and keep clear the land farms, mines and quarries need to expand. They also lean a little away from the harbors, airports, vehicle depots and freight stations you build.
  • Roundabouts (with Roundabouts & Interchanges): from about 1960 towns build some one-way roundabouts at their new crossings, most in France and the rest of Europe.

Settings

  • Act on (default New towns only): New towns only (on a new map, every town), All towns (existing towns too), or Off.
  • Vanilla town street angles (default Automatic (by region)): the street angles the game itself uses for towns: straight grids in North America and Oceania, more irregular in Europe and the Mediterranean. Grid, Mixed, Organic or Game default.
  • Starting town size (default Game size): Smaller or Small shrinks a new map's starting towns, so growth has more room to shape them. Pick it when you create the map.
  • Streets at industries (default Game default): Off leaves out the small streets the map generator lays at industries, so no house clusters grow next to the works. New maps only.
  • Town and stop names (default Landscape names): Landscape names or Game names. Other name mods keep their own names; the Asian, Chinese, Korean, Polish and Russian lists keep the game's names.
  • Town growth (default By deliveries): By deliveries or Game default (the game evens each town's mix out).
  • Satellite towns (default On): On, Residential only, Industrial only or Off.
  • Strong town characters (default On): On or Off.
  • Town style (default Automatic): Automatic follows the map's name list, or pick a region: North American grid, European organic, Mediterranean, Nordic, North American suburbs and grid, East Asian, Japanese or Mixed.
  • Town layout (default Organic): Very organic, Organic, Mixed, Gridded or Very gridded.
  • Growth lookahead (default Medium): how far ahead of demand new streets are built.
  • Debug log (default Off): writes every planning step to stdout.txt.

Notes

  • Best on a new map: landscape names, strong characters, satellite towns and the head start only happen there.
  • Safe to add to an existing save: by default only towns founded afterwards get new streets (pick All towns to include the others).
  • Everything the mod builds is plain base-game streets and towns, so it all stays if you remove the mod (the game warns you); towns then grow the game's way again. The mod steers growth by locking some streets against the town's own street building; those stay locked, and the town still builds houses along them.
  • The mod's street building is free: towns grow only while the game runs, and the streets it builds cost you nothing and belong to the town.
  • Roundabouts & Interchanges is optional; its roundabouts here are base-game streets and stay if you remove it.
  • Works with Left Hand Traffic: junctions get left-hand lanes. Towns build no roundabouts then (they would run the wrong way).
  • Add or remove mods only from the main menu, never with a save loaded.

Revision 7 (2026-10-20)

  • Fixed for PlayStation 5: the town window card no longer risks crashing the UI where the console's game lacks a part the PC version has.

Revision 6 (2026-10-20)

  • Changed: side streets leave a street at a proper angle (at least 60°), no more shallow branches peeling off and running back beside it.
  • Changed: no new streets squeezed in a few meters beside another for a long stretch, and a new parallel row of streets waits until the row next to it has houses.
  • Improved: streets that end close to each other connect more often, now also with a right-angle corner, and dead ends that sat empty for a while can join too.
  • Fixed: a town whose houses spread to a far-off group no longer lays streets far out into the countryside toward it.
  • Faster: the mod does much less work every frame, about half its previous cost.

Revision 5 (2026-10-19)

  • New: a street that ends close to another street now often connects to it, with a T or by joining two dead ends, instead of stopping just short.
  • New: towns lean a little away from the harbors, airports, vehicle depots, maintenance stations and freight stations you build. A gentle preference, not a ban.
  • Changed: blocks between parallel streets are a little wider (at least 44 m), so far fewer blocks hold only a single building or a sliver of tiny ones.
  • Changed: towns always keep at least a quarter of their growth residential, so they stay places where people live.
  • Fixed: medium and large streets are built only as continuous through routes, no more single wide pieces between small streets that sent cars on detours.
  • Fixed: new streets are always plain town streets, never tram, bus-lane, barrier or tree-lined ones, also where they continue a street you upgraded.
  • Fixed: name lists from other mods (e.g. German town lists) are recognized, so new satellite towns get names in that list's language.
