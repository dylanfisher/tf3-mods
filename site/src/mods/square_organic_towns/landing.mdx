Towns in Transport Fever 3 normally grow the same way everywhere. With Organic Towns, each town keeps growing
in a street pattern that fits its **region, site, terrain, era and size**, so one map gets several styles:
towns on big, flat, open land sprawl with wide grids, suburbs and superblocks, while coast, hill and valley
towns grow denser, winding streets.

## What it does

- **A street style per town**, picked from its site (flat and open, coast, river, hills, valley or crest) and
  its region: North American grid and suburb, European organic core and 19th-century extension, Mediterranean
  hill town and ensanche, Japanese dense, Nordic, and superblock. Suburbs and superblocks wait for 1950.
- **New streets as the town grows**, joined at their dead ends, in a town shape that fits the site: compact,
  along a valley or coast, crossroads, fingers, fan, twin, oval or ridge.
- **Towns follow the landscape**: along the hillside, the valley floor, the shore or the roads out of town,
  with small motifs where the land calls for them, such as a street zigzagging up a slope or a curved harbor
  front around a bay.
- **Shore towns, satellite villages, districts and industrial "Works" towns** grow out of the bigger towns,
  linked by country lanes, with meadows and fields kept between them.
- **Names that fit the map**: new towns, districts and stops get names that match their site and region.

It works on new maps and, if you choose, on the towns of an existing save.
